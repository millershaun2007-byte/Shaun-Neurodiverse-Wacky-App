Partnering With You for Healthy, Happy Kids

As a parent, I know that navigating the digital world is one of our biggest challenges. We worry about screen time, but we also see how technology can be an amazing tool for learning and creativity. This app was built to be an ally for families. This section is here to offer some gentle guidance and support, based on my own experiences and research.

Understanding Neurodiversity

Neurodiversity means that everyone’s brain is unique. Some people think, learn, or experience the world differently—and that’s a good thing! Neurodivergent kids might have ADHD, autism, dyslexia, or other ways of thinking. These differences bring creativity, new ideas, and special talents to our world.

Where Can Kids Go for Support?

  • Trusted Adults: Talk to a parent, teacher, school counselor, or family member if you need help or just want to share how you feel.
  • Clubs & Groups: Join after-school clubs, art/music groups, or sports teams to make friends and try new things.
  • Community Centers & Libraries: Many offer programs, quiet spaces, and fun activities for all kids.
  • Online Communities: (With adult supervision) There are safe online spaces for kids to connect and share their interests.

Helpful Activities (Beyond Apps)

  • Drawing, painting, or building things
  • Listening to or making music
  • Playing outside, exploring nature, or gardening
  • Reading books or comics
  • Writing stories, poems, or comics
  • Cooking or baking with family
  • Yoga, dance, or movement games

Talking About Emotions

  • It’s okay to feel all kinds of emotions—happy, sad, excited, worried, or mad.
  • Try using “I feel…” sentences to share your feelings (like “I feel proud when I finish a drawing” or “I feel nervous before a test”).
  • Draw a picture or use colors to show how you feel.
  • Ask for a hug, a break, or help if you need it.
  • Remember: Talking about feelings is a sign of strength, not weakness!

Celebrate Individuality!

Every child is unique and beautiful. Your differences are your superpowers! Be proud of who you are, and remember that you deserve to be celebrated just as you are.

All children are amazing—let’s lift each other up and make the world a kinder, more creative place!

Remember: Every child is different. What works for one may not work for another. The most important thing is to listen, love, and support your child’s unique journey.

A New Way to Think About Screen Time: Quality Over Quantity

The conversation around screen time often focuses on one thing: the clock. While time limits are important (and we're building tools to help with that!), a more helpful approach is to focus on the **quality** of the screen time.

Ask yourself these questions about your child's digital activities:

  • Is it Creative? Does it allow them to make, design, or build something new?
  • Is it Active? Does it engage their mind with problem-solving or critical thinking?
  • Is it Connecting? Does it help them connect with family or friends in a positive way?
  • Is it Calming? Does it help them regulate their emotions when they feel overwhelmed?

If the answer to these is yes, then that screen time is valuable. This app is designed to hit all of these points, providing a high-quality digital experience you can feel good about.

Building Healthy Digital Routines

Routines help children feel safe and secure, and digital habits are no different. Integrating screen time into a predictable daily flow can prevent many of the usual struggles.

Tips for a Healthy Routine:

  1. Create "Screen-Free" Zones & Times: The dinner table and bedrooms are great places to make screen-free. It's also helpful to have a "no screens" rule for the first hour of the morning and the last hour before bed.
  2. Balance is Key: For every period of screen time, try to have a period of "green time" (playing outside), "face time" (connecting with family), or "down time" (quiet, non-digital play).
  3. Use a Visual Timer: For many kids, especially neurodivergent ones, seeing how much time is left is less stressful than a sudden "time's up!" A visual timer can help them prepare for the transition.
  4. Plan the Transition: Give gentle warnings. "In 10 minutes, we'll be saving our creations and turning off the tablet for dinner." This respects their engagement and helps avoid meltdowns.

👨‍👩‍👧 Fun Activities to Do at Home! 🏠

Looking for screen-free fun with your kids? Try these engaging activities designed for neurodivergent learners and all children!

🏃 Movement Breaks

Get those bodies moving! Perfect for high energy moments or sensory breaks.

⭐ Star Jumps

Time: 2-5 minutes
Age: 3+
Difficulty: Easy

Jump and spread your arms and legs like a star! Try 5, 10, or 20 repetitions. Count together for extra math practice!

💃 Dance Party

Time: 3-10 minutes
Age: All ages
Difficulty: Easy

Put on favorite music and dance freely! No rules, just movement and joy. Great sensory input!

🐻 Animal Walks

Time: 5-10 minutes
Age: 3+
Difficulty: Easy-Medium

Bear crawl, crab walk, frog jump, bunny hop! Move around the house like different animals. Builds strength and coordination.

🎯 Freeze Dance

Time: 5-10 minutes
Age: 3+
Difficulty: Easy

Dance while music plays, freeze when it stops! Great for impulse control and listening skills.

🧘 Kids Yoga Poses

Time: 5-15 minutes
Age: 4+
Difficulty: Easy

Try tree pose, cat/cow, downward dog, and butterfly! Great for calming and body awareness.

🏃 Obstacle Course

Time: 15-30 minutes
Age: 3+
Difficulty: Medium

Use pillows, chairs, tape on floor to create a course. Jump, crawl, balance, and navigate!

⚖️ Balance Challenges

Time: 5-10 minutes
Age: 4+
Difficulty: Easy-Medium

Stand on one foot, walk on a line of tape, balance a bean bag on your head! Develops coordination.

👋 Simon Says

Time: 5-15 minutes
Age: 3+
Difficulty: Easy

Classic game with movements! "Simon says jump!" Improves listening and following directions.

💡 Sensory Tip:

Movement activities provide vestibular and proprioceptive input that many neurodivergent kids need for regulation. Let them move at their own pace!

🎨 Creative Crafts

Unleash creativity with hands-on art projects using materials you already have!

🦄 DIY Unicorn Horns

Time: 20-30 minutes
Age: 4+
Materials: Paper, glitter, glue, headband

Roll paper into cone, decorate with glitter and colors, attach to headband. Magical!

🦕 Dinosaur Footprints

Time: 15-25 minutes
Age: 3+
Materials: Paint, paper plates, paper

Cut paper plates into dinosaur foot shapes, dip in paint, stamp on paper. Roar-some art!

🎭 Paper Plate Masks

Time: 20-30 minutes
Age: 4+
Materials: Paper plates, markers, scissors

Cut eye holes, decorate as animals, superheroes, or anything! Great for dramatic play.

📦 Cardboard Creations

Time: 30-60 minutes
Age: 4+
Materials: Cardboard boxes, tape, markers

Build castles, spaceships, cars, houses! Open-ended creativity with recyclables.

🍃 Nature Collages

Time: 30-45 minutes
Age: 3+
Materials: Leaves, sticks, glue, paper

Collect natural items on a walk, glue to paper to create beautiful nature art!

🎨 Homemade Playdough

Time: 15 minutes
Age: 2+
Materials: Flour, salt, water, oil, food coloring

Recipe: 2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, 2 cups water, 2 tbsp oil. Mix, cook until thick. Add color!

🪨 Painted Rock Creatures

Time: 30-45 minutes
Age: 4+
Materials: Smooth rocks, paint, markers

Paint rocks to look like ladybugs, fish, monsters, or anything! Great for fine motor skills.

🧦 Sock Puppets

Time: 20-30 minutes
Age: 4+
Materials: Old socks, buttons, yarn, glue

Turn old socks into puppet friends! Add eyes, hair, and personality. Perfect for storytelling!

💡 Sensory Tip:

Crafts with different textures (glitter, glue, paint) provide tactile input. Let kids explore at their own pace and allow "messy play" when possible!

Age: 3+

"Find 10 red things!" or "Find 5 soft items!" Great for counting practice and color recognition.

👨‍🍳 Measuring While Cooking

Age: 4+

🔷 Pattern Making

Age: 3+

Use spoons, blocks, socks to make patterns: red-blue-red-blue or big-small-big-small!

⭕ Shape Hunt

Age: 3+

🗂️ Sorting Games

Age: 2+

🔢 Number Recognition

Age: 3+
Skills: Number recognition, counting

Find numbers on clocks, calendars, remote controls. Point them out and count together!

Math is everywhere! Point out numbers, shapes, and patterns throughout the day. Keep it playful and pressure-free.

Age: 4+

Age: 5+

Use magnetic letters, letter tiles, or write on paper. Build simple words: cat, dog, sun, fun!

Read together every day, even just 10 minutes! Follow your child's interests. Let them see you reading too!

🌈 Sensory-Friendly Adaptations

Making activities work for neurodivergent learners and all children!

🔊 Noise Levels

  • Offer quiet alternatives for loud activities
  • Use headphones or earmuffs if needed
  • Let kids control volume levels
  • Create "quiet zones" in your home

👐 Textures & Touch

  • Respect texture sensitivities (sticky, slimy, rough)
  • Offer tools (brushes, sponges) instead of hands
  • Let kids wear gloves for messy activities
  • Provide sensory bins with preferred textures

💡 Lights & Visual

  • Dim bright lights if they're overwhelming
  • Reduce visual clutter in play spaces
  • Use calming colors (blues, greens, purples)
  • Allow sunglasses indoors if helpful

⏰ Break Time Reminders

  • Build in movement breaks (every 15-20 min)
  • Watch for signs of overwhelm
  • It's okay to stop an activity early
  • Provide a calm-down space when needed

📅 Visual Schedules

  • Show pictures of activities in order
  • Help kids know what's coming next
  • Use timers for transitions
  • Stick to routines when possible

🎯 Individual Pace

  • Let kids work at their own speed
  • No pressure to "finish" activities
  • Celebrate small wins and efforts
  • Follow their lead and interests
